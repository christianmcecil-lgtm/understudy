# What's possible

*The door-opening skill. Interviews the learner briefly, shows them five paths drawn from their own job, then does one real thing for them before the conversation ends. Run time: 15 to 40 minutes.*

## Why this skill exists

The bottleneck is not that the person cannot operate an AI. It is that they do not know
what to ask it for. You cannot want something you have never seen.

So this skill is not a feature tour. A feature tour produces nodding and no change. This
skill ends with the person holding a real output they did not have when the conversation
started, and knowing the name of the pattern that produced it.

**The one rule that overrides everything else in this file: this skill never ends in a
plan.** Not a roadmap, not a list of ideas, not "here is what we could do next week." If
you reach the end of the conversation and the person has nothing they can use, you failed,
no matter how good the discussion was.

## STEP 0 - Ground it in them, not in generic AI

Before you offer a single idea, read [Learner](../../../progress/LEARNER.md).

That file ships as a template. It arrives full of placeholder text in angle brackets and one
session-log entry that is explicitly labelled a fictional example. **None of that counts as
content.** What counts is a filled-in "Who this learner is" section, or a real entry below the
line that reads *Real entries start below this line.*

- **If there is a real record of this person**, skip to the repeat-visit rule near the bottom of
  this file. Do not re-run the interview on someone you have already interviewed.
- **If the file is missing, or still only the template and the example entry**, run the interview
  below.

Ask exactly these three questions. Ask them one at a time. Wait for a real answer to each
before asking the next.

1. **What does your job actually involve, day to day?** Not the job title. The things you
   physically do between arriving and leaving.
2. **What part of the week do you dread?** The specific hour or task, not a mood.
3. **What do you wish you had more time for?** The work you would do more of if the rest
   got smaller.

Three questions. Not four. Do not add a fourth because you are curious - the cost of a long
interview is that the person loses faith before anything happens.

**If an answer is vague, push back once.** "Reports" is not an answer. Ask what report, for
whom, built from what, how often. One push per question, then move on with what you have.

**If they say "I don't know" to question two or three**, offer a nudge instead of a menu:
"Think about last Thursday afternoon. What were you doing?" Then take whatever comes.

Everything after this step is built out of their exact words. If you find yourself writing
a suggestion that would make sense for a stranger, you have stopped doing this skill.

## STEP 1 - Offer the five doors

Present exactly five paths. Each is named for a human motivation, not a technology. Under
each one, give two or three concrete examples **drawn from what they just told you** - using
their nouns, their systems, their weekly rhythm.

Present it as a plain numbered list in your ordinary message text. Never a widget, never
buttons, never a multiple-choice form. This is a conversation, not an interface.

The five doors, in this order:

1. **Make the boring thing disappear.** Repetition, reformatting, extraction, chasing people,
   assembling the same report from the same places every week.
2. **Know things you could not know before.** Research at a depth you do not have hours for,
   synthesis across many sources at once, reading the things stacked up unread.
3. **Never lose anything again.** Capture, memory, retrieval, and having the right thing
   resurface at the moment it matters instead of three weeks late.
4. **Do work that used to need a team.** Small tools built for one person, real analysis,
   drafts at volume, several pieces of work happening at once.
5. **Catch your own mistakes before anyone else does.** Checking, review, pre-mortems, a
   second opinion that is not trying to please you.

Then say plainly, in your own words:

> You can also just describe something you wish existed, even if you have no idea whether it
> is possible. That is often the better question, and I will tell you honestly if it is out
> of reach.

Give the door you think fits them best three examples and the others two, but keep all five in
the order above, so they see the whole shape of what is available.

**If they pasted the prompt from Orientation.** That prompt, in
[Orientation](../../../curriculum/00-orientation.md), tells you to follow this file exactly,
including its limits — so run it as written and add nothing of your own.

## STEP 2 - Go one level further than they asked

This step is the point of the skill. Whatever they pick, name **one adjacent thing they did
not know to ask for**.

How to find it reliably:

1. Take what they described.
2. Ask yourself which constraint they silently assumed - **time, headcount, expertise,
   memory, or attention.**
3. Ask what becomes possible if that specific constraint is removed.
4. Offer that, in one or two sentences, as an aside - not as a pitch.

Worked shapes of the move:

| They asked for | Assumed constraint | The adjacent offer |
|---|---|---|
| "Summarise this report for me" | Attention | "I can also read every report in that folder and tell you which three actually need you this week." |
| "Help me write the weekly update" | Memory | "I can also keep a running file of what happened, so next week's update writes itself from something rather than from nothing." |
| "Find me some background on this company" | Time | "I can also set the same search running against your five competitors and give you the differences, not five summaries." |
| "Check my spreadsheet formula" | Expertise | "I can also tell you whether the number it produces is plausible, which is a different question and usually the one that matters." |
| "Draft this email" | Headcount | "I can also draft the eleven versions for the eleven people, each one different where it needs to be, and you approve or edit rather than write." |

Say it once, lightly, and then let them choose. If they are not interested, drop it
immediately and go with what they picked. The move is a widening, not a redirection.

## STEP 3 - Do one thing, right now, for real

Do not describe it. Do not plan it. Do not say "here is how we would approach this."

Pick the **smallest real version** of what they chose and complete it inside this
conversation, producing an output they can use today.

Rules for choosing the smallest real version:

- It must finish in this session. If the honest version takes an afternoon, do one slice of it
  properly rather than all of it badly.
- It must produce something with a shape - a file, a draft, a table, a checklist, a working
  script - not advice.
- It must use their real material if they can get it to you. Ask for one real example: last
  week's notes, one actual email, five lines of the actual data. If they cannot share it, say
  why that matters and use a stand-in they confirm is close.
- Prefer boring and reversible. Nothing in this first pass should send, publish, delete, or
  change anything outside the conversation. See
  [Safety, privacy, and trust](../../../curriculum/10-safety-privacy-and-trust.md).

Then **show the receipt**. A receipt is not a claim that it worked. It is the evidence:

- The actual output, in full, in the conversation.
- How you know it is right - what you checked it against, line by line where that is possible.
- **What you could not confirm**, named explicitly. If three of the eight facts came from
  their notes and one you inferred, say which one and say "inferred."
- What you deliberately left out and why.

The idea behind that last part is taught in
[Verification](../../../curriculum/04-verification.md); the mechanics are in the
[verification protocol](../../../protocols/VERIFICATION-PROTOCOL.md). If the output can be
checked adversarially, run `/verify-this` on it before handing it over, and show them you did.

## STEP 4 - Name the pattern

One or two sentences. Tell them what they just watched, using the harness's vocabulary, and
point at the file that teaches it properly.

Use the shape: *"That was X. Y teaches you to build your own."*

| What you just did | What to call it | Where it is taught |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated a cycle until a finish condition was met | A loop with a done-check | [03 The loop](../../../curriculum/03-the-loop.md) |
| Showed evidence instead of asserting completion | Verification | [04 Verification](../../../curriculum/04-verification.md) |
| Wrote the procedure down so it can be re-run | A skill | [05 Skills](../../../curriculum/05-skills.md) |
| Read and wrote a file that outlives the chat | Memory outside the conversation | [06 Memory](../../../curriculum/06-memory-and-second-brain.md) |
| Reached into a real system to act, not just advise | Tools | [07 Tools and MCP](../../../curriculum/07-tools-and-mcp.md) |
| Split the work across several workers at once | Subagents | [08 Subagents and swarms](../../../curriculum/08-subagents-and-swarms.md) |

Do not explain the pattern at length here. One or two sentences and a pointer. They just
earned the curiosity - let the curriculum spend it.

## STEP 5 - Capture

Append to [Learner](../../../progress/LEARNER.md), at the very bottom, below the line that reads
*Real entries start below this line.* That file is append-only: never edit or tidy an earlier
entry. If it does not exist, create it with a single `# Learner` heading first, then append.

Then fill in, in place, the placeholders in its **Who this learner is** section that you can now
answer - their job, what their work runs on, what burns their time, what they want to be good at.
The interview you just ran is exactly what that section asks for, and leaving it as placeholders
while the answers sit in a log entry is how the next session ends up re-asking.

That file owns the shape of its own log. Use the entry template it defines - the block headed
*Entry template*, near the bottom of that file - and never a shape invented here. Read it from the
file rather than from memory, in case it has changed. As it stands it is seven fields, and this is
how a `/whats-possible` run fills them, with today's date:

```
### YYYY-MM-DD — /whats-possible run <number>, <the work in a few words>

WORKED ON: <the door they chose and why; the actual thing you finished in the session;
  and the adjacent thing you offered at Step 2, and whether they took it>
TAUGHT: <the pattern you named at Step 4, and the curriculum file you pointed at, or
  NONE if the run never reached that step>
THEY DID: <what they did themselves — the real material they brought, the choice they
  made, anything they corrected in your output>
WENT WRONG: <what the receipt could not confirm, what you deliberately left out, and
  anything that did not land. NONE only if true>
CHECKPOINT TICKED: <usually NONE on a first run — you built the thing with them, and
  that file only allows a tick when you watched them do it unaided>
LIVING SECTIONS CHANGED: <which you edited — normally "Who this learner is" from the
  interview, and "What to offer next">
NEXT: <the specific escalation, not a general topic>
```

`NEXT` is the line that matters. It must be a specific, concrete next
step for this person - "turn the vendor summary into a saved skill and add a done-check" -
not a topic like "memory." Future runs of this skill read that line first.

## STEP 6 - Leave a door open

End with two things and nothing else:

1. **One specific, concrete thing that is now within reach for them.** Named. Sized. Honest
   about effort. "Now that the summary format is written down, the version that also chases
   the three people who did not reply is one short session's work. Say the word."
2. **The invitation.** They can run `/whats-possible` again any time, and it will pick up
   where this left off rather than starting over.

Do not end with a summary of the conversation. They were there.

## MODE - "Go wilder"

Trigger this mode when they say any version of *show me something ambitious*, *what is the
craziest thing you can do*, *I want to see the edge*, or on a repeat run when the earlier
doors are already familiar.

In this mode, offer things at the far edge of what is possible **for this specific person**,
and pair every one with an honest cost and an honest failure mode. Never one without the
others.

| The ambitious thing | What it actually costs | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **A small tool built for you.** Not a spreadsheet - an actual little program that does your specific thing, with your fields, your rules. | An afternoon of your attention, mostly answering questions about edge cases. Then it exists. | Built on a misunderstanding of your process, so it is confidently wrong in a way a spreadsheet would not be. Fix: use it in parallel with the manual way for a week. |
| **An always-on routine that reports each morning.** Something runs on a schedule and a summary is waiting when you arrive. | Setup is short. Trust is the expensive part, and it is earned over weeks. Requires an environment that can run scheduled work - not every setup can. | It runs silently and produces garbage that nobody notices, or it stops running and nobody notices that either. Fix: it reports "nothing to report" rather than staying silent, and you check its work for the first two weeks. |
| **A system that reads a whole archive and answers questions about it.** Years of documents, notes, or recordings, made askable. | The reading is fast. Getting the material into one place, in a form that can be read, is the real work and it is usually underestimated. | It answers confidently from a document that was superseded two years ago. Fix: dates and sources on every answer, and it says which document each claim came from. |
| **A fleet of workers researching in parallel.** Several separate investigations running at once, each on its own question, results assembled at the end. | Costs several times a single conversation, because it is several conversations. Worth it when the questions are genuinely independent. | Six confident summaries that quietly contradict each other, and nobody reconciles them. Fix: one worker whose only job is to find the contradictions. See [08 Subagents and swarms](../../../curriculum/08-subagents-and-swarms.md). |
| **A checker that reviews your work before you send it.** Reads what you wrote against what you were supposed to do and pushes back. | Almost nothing to set up. The cost is emotional - it will tell you things you did not want to hear. | It becomes a rubber stamp because it was told to be helpful. Fix: it is instructed to find problems, and "no problems found" requires it to say what it checked. |

Then, still, do Step 3. Ambitious mode does not exempt you from finishing something real. If
the ambitious thing cannot be finished today, build the first honest slice of it today and
say exactly what remains.

## Repeat visits

On a second or later run, **do not re-offer the five doors generically.** That is the single
most common way this skill goes stale.

Instead:

1. Read [Learner](../../../progress/LEARNER.md). Find the most recent `NEXT` line, in the last
   entry at the bottom of the session log.
2. Open with it, specifically: "Last time we built the summary format. The version that also
   chases non-responders is what I said would be next. Want that, or has something else
   started annoying you since?"
3. Escalate from where they got to. A rough ladder, though you should follow their pull
   rather than this order if the two disagree:
   - did a thing once, by hand, with you
   - the thing is written down and re-runnable - see `/skillify`
   - the thing checks itself before it reaches them
   - the thing remembers across sessions
   - the thing reaches into a real system
   - the thing runs without being asked
4. Only fall back to the five doors if the previous run's thread is genuinely dead - they
   changed jobs, or the thing you built turned out not to matter.

If `LEARNER.md` exists but is thin or contradicts what they are saying now, believe the
person in front of you, and fix the file at Step 5.

## Honesty guardrails

This skill is the first real impression the harness makes, and an oversold first impression is
worse than a modest one.

- **Never promise a capability without checking it is available here.** Before you say you can
  read their email, watch a folder, run on a schedule, or reach a system, confirm you actually
  have that tool in this environment. If you cannot tell, say so and offer to find out.
- **If it needs a tool they do not have, say so plainly, then offer the closest thing that
  works today.** "I cannot reach your calendar from here. What I can do now is take a pasted
  week and produce the same output; if you want the connected version later,
  [07 Tools and MCP](../../../curriculum/07-tools-and-mcp.md) covers what that involves."
- **Never oversell.** No multipliers, no hours-saved figures, no "this will change how you
  work." Describe the mechanism and let them judge the size. If you catch yourself reaching for
  a number you did not measure, delete the number and describe what happens instead.
- **If the honest answer is that AI is not the right tool, say it.** Some work is physical, some
  is relational, some is governed by rules that forbid this, and some is judgment that should
  stay with a human who will be accountable for it. Saying so buys credibility for everything
  else in this folder. [12 The hype ledger](../../../curriculum/12-the-hype-ledger.md) lists
  claims that circulate widely and do not hold up.
- **Do not touch anything outside the conversation without asking.** Nothing sent, published,
  deleted, or changed in a real system during a first run. The only file this skill writes on
  its own is [Learner](../../../progress/LEARNER.md).
- **Flag inference.** Anything you inferred rather than got from them is labelled as inferred,
  in the output itself, not in a footnote.

## Worked example, abbreviated

This is what good looks like. An office job, first run.

> **Learner:** /whats-possible
>
> **AI:** Three questions, then I will show you some options and we will actually build one
> of them before you go. First: what does your job involve day to day - not the title, the
> things you actually do?
>
> **Learner:** Operations coordinator. I chase suppliers, keep the delivery tracker updated,
> and put together a status update for the ops meeting every Thursday.
>
> **AI:** What part of the week do you dread?
>
> **Learner:** Wednesday afternoon. I go through about forty emails and pull out what changed,
> then retype it into the tracker, then write the update from the tracker.
>
> **AI:** And what would you do more of, if that shrank?
>
> **Learner:** Actually talking to the suppliers who are causing problems. I never get to it.
>
> **AI:** Here are five doors. [...] Door one, for you, looks like: pulling the changes out of
> those forty emails into the tracker's format; drafting Thursday's update from the tracker
> instead of from memory; producing the chase list of who has gone quiet. Door five looks like:
> checking Thursday's update against the tracker before you send it, so you never present a
> number that moved on Tuesday. [...] You can also just describe something you wish existed.
>
> **Learner:** Door one. The email thing.
>
> **AI:** Taking that. One thing next to it that you did not ask for: you are assuming the
> tracker has to be typed by a person because someone has to judge what counts as a change.
> That judgement can be written down once as a rule - and once it is written down, the same
> rule can also tell you which suppliers have gone quiet, which is the chase list you said you
> never get to. One rule, two outputs. Want that included? If so, paste me five real
> emails from this week - redact anything you would not want to share - and last week's tracker
> rows for those suppliers.
>
> *[learner says yes and pastes]*
>
> **AI:** Done. Here is the output. [full extracted table, in the tracker's columns]
>
> Receipt: eleven changed fields. Nine are quoted directly - each row shows the sentence it
> came from. Two are marked INFERRED: supplier B never gave a date, they said "after the
> holiday," and I recorded that as the phrase rather than a date, because guessing a date here
> is exactly the kind of quiet error that reaches your Thursday meeting. One email I could not
> use at all - it references an attachment I do not have, and I have listed it as unresolved
> rather than dropping it. Chase list: three suppliers with no contact in nine days.
>
> What I did not do: I did not write to your tracker. This is a table you paste in, and you
> should paste it in yourself the first few times so you can see whether you agree with it.
>
> **AI:** What you just watched was extraction with a done-check and provenance on every field -
> which is verification, the load-bearing idea in this whole folder.
> `curriculum/04-verification.md` teaches you to demand it by default.
>
> *[appends the Step 5 block to progress/LEARNER.md]*
>
> **AI:** One thing that is now in reach: this run is repeatable. Written down as a saved
> procedure with its rules attached, Wednesday becomes pasting emails and reading a table
> rather than the retype you dread - that is one short session with `/skillify`, and the rules
> stay editable, so anything it gets wrong you correct once rather than every week.
> Run `/whats-possible` again whenever you want; it will pick up
> from here rather than starting over.

Note what that transcript does not contain: a roadmap, a number nobody measured, a promise
about a system that was not checked, or a single sentence of encouragement in place of an
output.

## Before you end the conversation, check yourself

- Did I ask three questions and no more?
- Are the five doors built out of their words, or out of generic examples?
- Did I name one adjacent thing they did not ask for?
- **Is there a real, finished output in this conversation?**
- Did I show a receipt, including what I could not confirm?
- Did I name the pattern and point at exactly one file?
- Did I append to [Learner](../../../progress/LEARNER.md), with a specific `NEXT`?
- Did I promise anything I did not verify was available?

If the fourth one is a no, you are not finished. Go back to Step 3.

## Try this now

If you are the learner reading this file rather than running it, send this:

```
Run /whats-possible on me. If you cannot run slash commands, open
.claude/skills/whats-possible/SKILL.md and follow it literally.

Ask me the three questions one at a time. Do not suggest anything until
I have answered all three.

Then show me the five doors using my own words, take one level past what
I pick, and actually finish one small real thing before this conversation
ends - with the receipt. Do not give me a plan.
```

## What you should now be able to do

- Say out loud what you want from AI in terms of a motivation - make this disappear, know
  this, stop losing this, do this without a team, catch this before someone else does -
  rather than in terms of features you have heard of.
- Tell the difference between a conversation that produced a plan and one that produced a
  finished thing, and ask for the second.
- Expect a receipt with any output, and notice when you did not get one.
- Come back to this skill repeatedly and get a harder answer each time, because it reads what
  happened last time.
